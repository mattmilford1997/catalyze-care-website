Healthcare Digital Marketing Metrics, Defined

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 27, 2026·8 min read

Healthcare digital marketing metrics are the numbers a practice uses to judge SEO, paid ads, and referral outreach. The most useful ones count patients: booked appointments, show rate, and cost per booked appointment, tied back to the channel that produced them. Platform numbers like impressions and clicks explain why those counts moved. Fifteen common report terms, defined in plain English and grouped by what they measure, make any agency report easier to read.

In one sentenceJudge healthcare digital marketing on booked appointments by source, and use impressions, clicks, and platform conversions to explain the change.

Metrics that count patients

Booked appointment

An inquiry that became a scheduled visit, counted by your intake team or practice management system. The number every other metric should roll up to.

Cost per booked appointment

Marketing spend for a period divided by booked appointments from that channel in the same period. State whether media, management fees, or both are included.

Patient acquisition cost

Total marketing cost divided by new patients who completed a first visit. It runs higher than cost per booked appointment because some bookings cancel or no-show.

Speed to lead

Time from a call, form, or fax arriving to the first response from your practice. Slow replies waste paid clicks and referrals alike.

Show rate

Share of booked appointments that the patient attended. Track it by source to see which channels bring patients who follow through.

Search and ad metrics

Impressions

How many times your listing, page, or ad appeared in search results or a feed. It counts appearances, so treat it as a visibility signal.

Clicks and click-through rate

Clicks are visits from a result or ad. Click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions, useful for testing titles and ad copy.

Average position

The average rank of your pages for a search in Google Search Console. Watch the trend for searches that matter to your services.

Business Profile actions

Calls, direction requests, and website clicks from your Google Business Profile, reported in its Performance view.

Platform conversion

An event the ad platform counts, such as a form submit or a call over a set length. It measures intent, and intake confirms whether it became a booking.

Qualified call

A tracked call that reached scheduling and matched a service you offer. Define the rule with intake before launch.

Referral source capture

Whether intake records where each new patient came from, such as a referring office, search, or an ad. Without it, channel reports are guesses.

Referral and answer-engine metrics

Active referring office

An office that sent at least one referral in the reporting period. Compare active offices month to month, split into new and returning.

Close-the-loop time

Days from receiving a referral to sending the referring office a status update. Fast updates keep offices sending patients.

AI Overview citation

When a Google AI Overview links to your page as a source. Check your most important searches by hand each month.

Which metrics belong in a monthly report

Put booked appointments by source, show rate, and cost per booked appointment at the top. Add speed to lead and active referring offices next, since your team can act on both. Keep impressions, clicks, and average position lower down as the explanation. FAQ

What is the most important healthcare digital marketing metric?

Booked appointments by source, counted by your intake team. Other metrics such as impressions, clicks, and platform conversions explain why that number moved.

What is the difference between a conversion and a booked appointment?

A conversion is an event the ad platform counts, such as a form submit or call. A booked appointment is a scheduled visit confirmed by intake. Only some conversions become bookings.

How can a practice track marketing metrics without sending PHI to ad platforms?

Use generic thank-you pages for conversion events, keep condition names out of URLs and form fields shared with ad platforms, and match bookings to sources inside your own intake system.
