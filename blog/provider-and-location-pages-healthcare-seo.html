Provider and Location Pages That Rank

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 27, 2026·8 min read

Provider and location pages answer the two questions patients ask before they book: who will I see, and where. In SEO for healthcare practices, these are often the pages Google shows for a specialty plus a city or for a doctor's name, so each one needs its own clear title, real details that match your Google Business Profile, and a way to book from a phone. When a referring office hands a patient a name, the patient usually searches that name next, and a clear provider page is what they find.

In one sentenceGive every staffed location and every provider a page with real details, matching schema, and links to each other, and skip near-identical city pages for towns without an office.

What a strong location page includes

  • City in the title and heading. For example, Pediatric Therapy in Round Rock.
  • Address, phone, and hours that match the Business Profile for that office.
  • Services offered at this office. Groups often offer different services at different sites.
  • Providers who practice here, each linked to their own page.
  • Practical details. Parking, transit, accessibility, and insurance accepted.
  • A booking button and a map near the top on mobile.

Write each location page from scratch. A paragraph copied across five offices with only the city swapped gives Google little reason to rank any of them.

What a strong provider page includes

  • Full name, credentials, and specialty in the heading.
  • Board certification or licensure, with a link to the public verification source where one exists.
  • Locations and days the provider sees patients.
  • Services and age groups in plain language.
  • Languages spoken and whether new patients are welcome.
  • A recent photo and a booking link.

Provider pages also help your healthcare referral marketing. Referring offices and patients both look up the clinician by name, and a page with credentials and booking details turns that search into an appointment.

Schema that matches the page

Structured data helps search engines read who you are. Use MedicalClinic or a similar LocalBusiness type on each location page and Physician on provider pages, and mark up only details a visitor can see. Google explains the fields in its local business structured data guide. Test each page in the Rich Results Test after publishing.

One page per staffed location

Create location pages for places where you have a staffed office. For nearby towns you serve without an office, one service-area section on the main location page is cleaner than a stack of thin city pages. This mirrors the one-profile-per-location rule for Google Business Profile, covered in local SEO for multi-provider groups.

How to connect the pages

  1. Link locations to providers. Each location page lists the providers who work there.
  2. Link providers back to locations. Each provider page names the offices and days they see patients.
  3. Link services to locations. Each service links to the offices that offer it.
  4. Point each Business Profile to its location page. The website link on every profile opens the matching office page.

FAQ

Should every provider have their own page?

Yes, when they see patients under their own name. Patients and referring offices search by clinician name, and a dedicated page with credentials, locations, and a booking link answers that search.

Should a practice create a page for every city it serves?

Create location pages for places with a staffed office. For nearby towns without an office, one service-area section on the main location page is cleaner than many near-identical pages.

Which schema should a healthcare practice use on these pages?

MedicalClinic or another LocalBusiness type on location pages and Physician on provider pages, with every marked-up detail visible on the page.
