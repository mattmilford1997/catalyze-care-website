Healthcare Paid Ads Compliance Checklist for 2026

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 29, 2026·10 min read

A healthcare paid ads compliance checklist for 2026 walks a practice owner through three gates before spend scales: whether creative and certification match Google and Meta healthcare rules (including LegitScript when the category requires it), whether pixels and conversion events keep PHI out of ad platforms, and whether phone calls longer than 60 seconds are measured as real booked-inquiry signals. In one sentenceConfirm LegitScript or other certification when your category needs it, keep diagnosis-level data out of pixels and thank-you URLs, and count phone calls longer than 60 seconds as conversions before you raise budget.

1. Confirm certification and account verification first

Some healthcare categories cannot run ads until a third-party certification is approved. Addiction treatment (US and Canada), online pharmacies, and many telehealth practices that prescribe online need LegitScript or an equivalent path. Health insurance uses Google's own certification. Most general medical and specialty practices without prescribing online only need standard Google advertiser verification, but you should confirm the category before you write ads.

Google publishes current rules in its healthcare and medicines policy. Meta restricts personalized advertising that uses health information. Catalyze Care scopes certification needs during the audit, before media spend starts. Longer guide: Google Ads for healthcare in 2026 and LegitScript.

2. Write LegitScript-aware creative (and keep claims approved)

Creative that survives review describes the service, location, insurance accepted, and how to book. It avoids personalized health language, condition-based promises, and before-and-after claims the practice cannot support in writing. Landing page headlines should match the ad so the policy review sees one story.

  • Allowed pattern. "Book a consultation with our cardiology clinic in San Diego" with hours and a phone CTA.
  • Risky pattern. Copy that implies the reader has a diagnosis, or that the ad was chosen because of a health condition.
  • Landing match. The page names the same service as the ad, shows a mobile phone button, and uses a generic thank-you URL with no condition in the path.

3. Keep PHI out of pixels and audience tools

HHS OCR's tracking technology guidance explains that pixels can involve PHI when they connect a person to a health-related page or action. Practical rules Catalyze Care follows on paid campaigns:

  • Place conversion tags on public marketing pages and generic thank-you URLs, not on patient portals or diagnosis-specific success pages.
  • Strip diagnosis, medication, member ID, and free-text clinical fields from any event payload sent to Google or Meta.
  • Build remarketing audiences from public page visits only. Exclude converters with non-PHI signals when the platform allows it.
  • List every tag on the site before launch so a leftover pixel is not still firing from an old vendor.

4. Measure conversion events that match booked inquiries

Clicks alone are not the product. Catalyze Care aims campaigns at booked appointments the front desk can confirm. Set conversion events that reflect that goal.

  1. Form submit on a generic thank-you URL. Fire once when intake receives a complete inquiry, with no condition in the path.
  2. Phone calls longer than 60 seconds. Use call extensions or a call tracking number the practice controls, and count only calls that last more than 60 seconds so brief hang-ups stay out of the conversion column.
  3. Click-to-call from mobile when duration can be measured. Prefer the same 60-second floor when the platform reports call length.
  4. Monthly report fields. Spend, policy status, and booked-inquiry counts from intake. Impressions without a booked-inquiry column are noise.

5. Landing page and intake readiness before you scale

A compliant campaign still wastes media if the click lands on a homepage maze. Before raising budget, confirm:

  • Approved claims only, reviewed by the practice.
  • Phone CTA visible without scrolling on a phone-sized screen.
  • Forms limited to fields intake can process the same day.
  • Thank-you URL is generic (for example /thank-you), with no specialty or condition slug.
  • Privacy policy linked in the footer and available from the landing page.
