Your 2027 Healthcare Marketing Plan on One Page

By Andrew · Updated September 9, 2026·3 min read

A one-page 2027 healthcare marketing plan has five parts: a numeric target, three named channels with owners and budgets, five monthly numbers you can check against intake, a calendar, and a compliance line. Plug in published Catalyze Care fees where they apply—$5,000/month referral (3-month minimum), $799/month SEO, $2,500/month paid ads management (media separate)—then measure your own booked-patient denominator. In one sentenceWrite a target, three channels, five numbers, a calendar, and a compliance line—then review monthly against your own intake, not an invented multiple.

Section 1: The target

One sentence. "In 2027 we will add [N] new active patients per month by December, at a blended cost per active patient of $[X] or less."

Derive N from capacity (how many patients can your clinicians actually see) and X from lifetime value (what a patient is worth in year one, and what share of that you're willing to spend to get them). If you can't fill in both numbers, that's the first task.

Section 2: The three channels

Every practice we've seen grow sustainably runs three channels with rigor. Not eight channels casually.

Referrals. Target: [N] referring offices by year end, [M] referred patients per month. Actions: quarterly outreach waves to [segments], nurture cadence for existing referrers, closed-loop notes at 95 percent. Owner: [name]. Budget: $[X].

Paid. Target: [N] booked patients per month at $[X] cost per booked. Actions: Google search for intent, Meta for awareness and parent-decided care, compliant landing pages and measurement, January launch. Owner: [name]. Budget: $[X] per month.

Organic and directories. Target: rank for "[service] [state]" in [N] states, [N] patients per month from directories. Actions: state and city pages, facts page, schema, Google Business Profile, Psychology Today or specialty directory optimization, monthly content. Owner: [name]. Budget: $[X].

If you only have capacity for two, drop paid last for a practice with a strong referral base, and drop referrals last for a practice launching in a new market with no relationships.

Section 3: The five numbers

Reported monthly, on one page, reviewed by leadership:

  1. New active patients (completed first visit, still in care) by channel
  2. Cost per active patient by channel, from your own intake data
  3. Referring offices active in the trailing 90 days, with new and fading counts
  4. Speed to first visit (median days from inquiry to appointment)
  5. Intake conversion (inquiries to booked, booked to seen)

Numbers 4 and 5 are operational, not marketing, and they're on the marketing dashboard because they determine whether the marketing works.

Section 4: The calendar

  • January: paid campaigns live day one; Medicaid coverage re-verification in intake; referral capacity update.
  • March: first quarterly review against the five numbers; cut what's not working.
  • April: referral wave two; capacity update.
  • June: mid-year review; reallocate budget to the best-performing channel.
  • August: back-to-school referral wave (pediatric practices); capacity update.
  • October: Q4 prep: directory audit, insurance content refresh, January campaign build.
  • December: year-end review; write the 2028 version of this page.

Section 5: The compliance line

One line acknowledging the constraints: paid campaigns run against current Google and Meta health policies with required certifications; tracking is set up to avoid transmitting PHI; outreach follows TCPA and fax rules with maintained suppression lists; all vendors touching patient data have BAAs. If any of these isn't true today, it's a January task.

That's the plan

Target, three channels with owners and budgets, five numbers, a calendar, a compliance line. Fits on a page. Reviewed monthly. Most practices that grow have something like this; most that don't, don't.
