Local SEO for Multi-Provider Groups Without Duplicate GBP Listings

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 24, 2026·10 min read

Multi-provider groups avoid duplicate Google Business Profile (GBP) listings by keeping one profile per real-world location, confirming ownership, locking a primary category, and routing reviews to that location. Individual clinicians usually appear as providers on the location profile or on the practice site, not as separate map pins for the same address. Service: SEO for healthcare practices. Ownership primer: Google Business Profile for healthcare practices.

In one sentenceOne GBP per location, one owner, one primary category, and a named review path. Extra pins for the same address create conflict, not coverage.

What goes wrong with multi-provider listings

Groups often create a second GBP when a new clinician joins, or when a marketer wants a doctor-name pin. Google then shows conflicting hours, categories, or phones for the same suite. Patients call the wrong number. Reviews scatter. Local pack eligibility suffers. Ownership contrast: Google Business Profile vs website SEO ownership.

Ownership and access

  • One primary owner. Practice administrator or marketing lead holds owner access. Agency partners get manager access you can revoke.
  • No orphaned claims. When a vendor ends, recover ownership before the next hire.
  • Match NAP fields to the door. Name, address, and phone must match what patients see on the building. Catalyze Care does not publish a street NAP in the site footer; your profile must match your real location.

Categories, services, and providers

Pick one primary category that matches what patients search. Add secondary categories sparingly. List services the location actually offers. Provider bios belong on the website and, where the product allows, as providers on the location profile. Do not open a second GBP for each clinician at the same address.

Review routing without duplicate pins

Send patients to the location GBP after a visit. Name who replies and in what window. Do not claim a star rating or review volume target you cannot show. Reputation workflow: review generation and reputation for healthcare practices.

Multi-location and telehealth notes

Each brick-and-mortar location gets its own GBP. Pure telehealth without a patient-facing address follows Google's current rules for service-area businesses. Multi-state nuance: local SEO for multi-state telehealth. Pack vs organic: local pack vs organic SEO for healthcare.

How to clean duplicate GBPs

  1. Inventory every listing. Search the practice name, phone, and address. Screenshot conflicts.
  2. Keep the accurate location profile. Merge or request removal for duplicates at the same address.
  3. Confirm owner access. Move agency staff to manager. Document recovery email.
  4. Lock category and review path. One primary category. One reply owner. Then resume posts and Q&A.

FAQ

Should every clinician get their own Google Business Profile?

Not for the same physical address. Keep one GBP per real-world location. Clinicians appear as providers on that profile or on the practice site. Separate pins for the same suite create duplicate listings.

How do multi-provider groups avoid duplicate GBP listings?

Confirm a single owner, match name and phone to the door, lock one primary category, and remove or merge extra profiles at the same address before adding posts or ads.

Where do reviews go in a multi-provider group?

Route reviews to the location GBP. Name who replies. Do not open a second profile just to chase more review URLs.
