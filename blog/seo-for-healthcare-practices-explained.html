Definition

Patients type specialty plus place: “child psychiatrist Denver Medicaid,” “speech therapist near me,” “ABA therapy Richmond.” A practice that does not appear for those queries is invisible at the moment of intent. Local pack, Maps, and organic results all matter. For multi-state telehealth, state and city pages do the same job without a waiting room. See local SEO for multi-state telehealth.

Healthcare SEO is still SEO. Google’s public guidance on creating helpful, reliable, people-first content is the same foundation used for any site that wants to appear in Search. Healthcare pages sit in a category Google has long treated as higher-stakes (health information that can affect a person’s decisions). That is a reason to write accurate, medically reviewed pages — not a reason to stuff keywords or invent outcome claims.

Source: Google Search Central, Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content.

Local SEO and the practice listing

For most brick-and-mortar or hybrid practices, the Google Business Profile is the first ranking surface that matters. It feeds the local pack and Maps. Google documents how to create and manage a Business Profile, including name, category, hours, phone, service areas, and attributes. The listing should match how the practice actually operates — not a keyword-stuffed name, and not hours the front desk does not keep.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, How to create a Business Profile.

Local work Catalyze Care includes, in the same language as the SEO page:

  • Google Business Profile — name, primary and secondary categories, hours, phone, services, photos, and a review path that matches operations.
  • Citations and directories — consistent name, address, phone, and insurance facts across healthcare directories. Inconsistency is how two “practices” appear for one office. See directory profile audit.
  • Service-area honesty — if the practice is telehealth-only in five states, the listing and the site should say that. Do not pin a map pin in a city with no location.

Reviews are part of local SEO. They are also a compliance surface: do not incentivize reviews that reveal another patient’s condition, and do not reply in a way that confirms someone is a patient. That is practice policy, not a ranking trick.

Directory work is unglamorous and it is where most practice sites leak. A Healthgrades, Psychology Today, insurance directory, or hospital “find a doctor” listing that still shows last year’s phone number or a closed location will outrank a new service page for a while, because patients and referring coordinators trust the listing they already use. The job is consistency: same legal name (or the name patients actually search), same address format, same booking path. If the practice is telehealth-only, the listing should say service area and not invent a lobby. If the practice has two offices, each office needs its own honest listing — not one profile pretending to cover both cities.

Google’s Business Profile help is the public source of truth for what the listing can contain. This guide will not invent a “local pack guarantee” or a citation-count target. The test is operational: can a patient call the number, arrive at the address, and book the service the listing names?

Practice-site basics

The website has to answer the same query the listing answers, in language a patient and a referring coordinator can use. Google’s structured-data documentation is explicit that markup must match visible content. Inventing FAQ schema that is not on the page, or MedicalOrganization markup that names credentials the site does not show, is the wrong kind of technical SEO.

Source: Google Search Central, Introduction to structured data markup in Google Search.

On-page

  • Service pages that match the query. One page for “pediatric occupational therapy” that states ages, insurance, locations or states, and how to book. Not a blog post that never names the city.
  • Titles and headings that a human would type. Specialty + place + constraint (Medicaid, ages, telehealth) when those are true.
  • Internal links between services, locations, and the referral or booking path so crawlers and patients can find the next page.

Technical

  • Indexable HTML. Important copy is in the page, not only in an image or a blocked script.
  • Mobile and Core Web Vitals. A parent on a phone is the default visitor. Slow booking widgets lose the visit SEO earned.
  • Crawl health. Clean redirects, a current sitemap, and robots.txt that still allows Googlebot — and, on this site, major AI crawlers. See this site’s robots.txt.
  • Schema that matches the page. MedicalOrganization, Physician, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, and Speakable only where the visible copy exists.

Medically reviewed content

Pages written for patients and referring offices, not keyword filler. A clinician or designated reviewer should be able to stand behind what the page claims. Catalyze Care does not publish invented outcome percentages to “win” a featured snippet. If a page discusses a condition, it should be accurate and current; if it is a marketing page, it should say who the practice sees and how to start.

Google’s helpful-content documentation is explicit that people-first pages outperform pages written mainly to rank. For healthcare, that also means not overstating what the practice treats, not copying another clinic’s service list, and not publishing “10 signs you have [condition]” posts that a clinician would not sign. Those pages may attract clicks. They also create a medical and advertising-policy problem. A practice site should be able to answer: who we see, where (or in which states), what insurance or self-pay looks like at a high level, how to refer or book, and what happens after the first contact.

Referring offices need a different paragraph than patients. Coordinators look for fax numbers, ages, insurance constraints, and access (how soon a new patient can be seen). If that information lives only in a PDF the office manager emailed last year, the site is not doing its job. Put the referral path on the service page. Link it from the homepage. Keep it in the same specialty language the outreach fax uses — that is how SEO and referral marketing stay on one map.

What this guide will not do: quote a national average ranking timeline, a “first-page in 90 days” promise, or a traffic multiple. Organic work is judged in months. Some markets move faster because the listing was the only broken piece. Some do not, because every competitor already has a complete profile and a decade of reviews. The audit on the SEO page is where that difference shows up — before a retainer starts.

AI Overviews and answer engines

People also ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google’s AI Overviews “who is a good [specialty] near me.” Google Search Central’s public guide on AI features says AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same foundational SEO as classic Search. To be eligible as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet. There are no extra technical requirements unique to AI Overviews. Google lists the same practices: allow crawling, make content findable through internal links, keep important text actually in the HTML, and make sure structured data matches visible text.

Source: Google Search Central, AI features and your website.

What that means here, at a high level:

  • There is no separate “AI SEO” product that replaces local listings, service pages, and accurate NAP.
  • Clear definitions help. A speakable one-sentence definition and an FAQ that matches visible copy are useful for people and for systems that quote a page. They are not a guarantee of an AI Overview citation.
  • llms.txt is a convenience file this site publishes so answer engines can find the living index. It is not a Google ranking factor.
  • Do not block the crawlers you want. This site’s robots.txt allows GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, Applebot-Extended, meta-externalagent, and others. Blocking them and then asking why AI tools cannot cite the practice is inconsistent.

This guide will not claim a percentage of “AI Overview wins.” That number, if a vendor quotes one, is not a Catalyze Care metric.

When SEO vs paid vs referral first

SEO captures patients who are already searching. It compounds. It is the wrong first channel when the practice has empty chairs this month and no time for pages to accrue.

  • SEO first — patients already search the specialty and city; the listing or site is missing or inconsistent. Published fee: $799 / month.
