Google Business Profile for Healthcare Practices

By Andrew · Published September 10, 2026·9 min read

A Google Business Profile (GBP) is the listing Google uses to show a healthcare practice on Maps, the local pack, and the knowledge panel. For a brick-and-mortar or hybrid clinic, a citeable GBP is one that matches the real name, address, phone, hours, category, and services patients will find at the door and on the website. Google’s own Help and Search Central docs treat that consistency as the foundation of local visibility. This guide covers what to publish, what to keep aligned, and how multi-location practices should treat one profile per location. Catalyze Care SEO is published at $799/month. Six steps to a citeable GBP

  1. Create or claim the profile for the staffed location. Google’s Help documents how to create a Business Profile and how representation guidelines apply. Do not invent an address to “get a pin.”
  2. Set category, services, and appointment URL. Primary category should match what patients actually ask for. Secondary categories are extras, not a keyword dump. Point the appointment URL at a page the practice owns.
  3. Match name, address, and phone. Suite numbers and tracking numbers are the usual mismatch. Pick one public phone and use it on the website, GBP, and major directories.
  4. Publish real hours and photos. Hours should match the door, including holiday exceptions. Photos should show the real clinic, parking, or waiting room—not stock-only imagery.
  5. Manage Q&A and reviews without PHI. Answer Q&A with facts (ages, payers, parking). Respond to reviews without naming a patient or a condition. Do not incentivize reviews in ways that violate Google’s policies.
  6. Measure what intake can confirm. Calls, direction requests, form submits, and booked appointments. Vanity impressions are not a booked visit.

One profile per staffed location

Google’s representation guidelines expect each location patients can visit to be represented accurately. A second office with its own suite and hours gets its own profile. A shared waiting room with two legal entities still needs an honest name and address—do not merge them into a keyword-stuffed title. Multi-location vs single-site detail: multi-location vs single-site healthcare SEO.

Service-area-only practices (no public visit address) follow a different GBP rule set. Do not hide an address that patients actually use, and do not publish an address no one staffs.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, Guidelines for representing your business on Google.

Reviews, PHI, and tracking

A review response that confirms a named patient’s visit or condition is a disclosure problem, not a “reputation tactic.” HHS OCR has published guidance on online tracking technologies used by covered entities; thank-you pages and review widgets should not leak identifiers plus health context into pixels or URL parameters. This page is educational, not legal advice.

Source: HHS OCR, Use of Online Tracking Technologies. Source: HHS, HIPAA and marketing.

What schema should match

If the practice publishes MedicalOrganization or LocalBusiness markup, the visible name, address, and phone on the page must match the profile. Schema that invents a street address this site does not have—or that the practice does not use—is a mismatch, not an SEO upgrade.
