Local SEO for Multi-State Telehealth

November 2, 2026·4 min read

Patients search for care locally even when the care is delivered virtually. "Speech therapist near me," "psychiatrist in Richmond," "ABA therapy Denver." A telehealth practice that ranks for none of those is invisible to most of its market. Here's how to fix that.

State pages are the foundation

For a practice licensed in multiple states, one page per state is the minimum. Each should be a real page, not a template with the state name swapped:

  • Which services are available in that state (they often differ by licensure)
  • Which insurance plans and which Medicaid managed care plans you accept there
  • Which clinicians are licensed there, with bios
  • State-specific logistics: how telehealth consent works, how prescriptions are handled if applicable, wait times
  • Local context: major cities, common referral pathways, anything a patient in that state would want to know

Thin state pages get ignored by Google and by patients. Substantive ones rank for "[service] [state]" and support the city pages beneath them.

City pages, carefully

City pages under each state ("speech therapy Richmond VA") can rank well, but only if they're differentiated. A hundred identical city pages is a doorway-page problem. Limit to cities where you have real demand, and give each page something unique: local referral partners, local school districts or health systems, clinician availability, a testimonial from that area.

Google Business Profile without an office

Google Business Profile is the single biggest local ranking factor, and it's built for physical locations. Telehealth practices have options:

  • Service-area business. GBP allows businesses that serve customers at their location (or virtually) to hide the address and define a service area. Many telehealth practices qualify. The service area can span multiple states, but ranking is still anchored to the address on file.
  • A real address per state. Virtual offices and coworking addresses are a gray area; Google's guidelines require that the business be staffed at the address during listed hours, and profiles using virtual offices get suspended regularly. Where a practice does have staff at a location, even part-time, a profile there is legitimate and powerful.
  • Practitioner profiles. Individual clinicians can have their own GBP listings tied to a practice location. For a practice with clinicians in different states, this can be a compliant way to establish presence where clinicians actually work.

Do not create profiles at addresses where no one works. Suspensions are hard to reverse and can affect the main listing.

Directories that actually send patients

Directory listings matter for two reasons: they're a ranking signal (consistent name, address, phone across sources), and some of them are where patients actually search.

For behavioral health, Psychology Today remains a major direct source of patients and should be treated as a paid channel with optimized clinician profiles. For medical specialties, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Vitals, and WebMD. For pediatric therapy, specialty directories and Medicaid plan provider directories, which parents use directly. Insurance directories are also where referring offices verify you're in network, so an out-of-date entry costs referrals, not just SEO.

Claim everything, keep it consistent, and put real effort into the two or three that drive volume for your specialty.

Content that supports local rankings

Blog content keyed to state-specific questions ("Does Virginia Medicaid cover speech therapy?" "How to get a psychiatry referral in Colorado") builds authority for the state pages and captures patients earlier in the search process. Link it to the state page. Publish on a cadence.

Technical hygiene

Schema markup for medical organization and each clinician (with license state), fast mobile pages, a sitemap that includes every state and city page, and a clean internal linking structure from homepage to state to city to service. Most telehealth sites we audit are missing at least two of these.
