Why groups get this wrong

A four-location group often has a practice manager who edits hours, a marketing coordinator who edits the website, and an agency that posts to GBP. When those three people use different phone numbers or different “who we take” lines, Google and patients treat the listings as unreliable. Google’s own Business Profile help still expects accurate, current information that matches what a visitor finds on your site.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, guidelines for representing your business.

Roles that should be written down

  1. Keep primary logins at the practice. The group owns the Google account and the CMS. Invite the agency as a manager on GBP and as an editor on pages you approve. Do not make the agency the only owner.
  2. Name one operations owner for NAP. Hours, phone, address, and who you take change at the front desk. That person is the source of truth for GBP and the website footer.
  3. Name one marketing owner for posts and pages. GBP posts, photos, and service-page copy sit with marketing. They publish only facts operations already confirmed.
  4. Assign review replies. One person replies. Thank the reviewer. Invite a private follow-up. Do not put diagnosis-level detail in a public reply. Reviews workflow: review generation and reputation for healthcare practices.

NAP and one profile per staffed location

Use one Google Business Profile per staffed location patients can visit. Do not open a profile per provider unless Google’s current category rules for that specialty require a separate listing. Name, address, and phone on GBP, the website, and citations must match. Checklist: local SEO checklist for healthcare practices.
