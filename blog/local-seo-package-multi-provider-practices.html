By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 18, 2026·10 min read

In one sentenceOne GBP per staffed location, matching citations, honest on-page copy, and a reviews path the practice controls.

Google Business Profile, one per staffed location

A multi-provider group is not one pin with every clinician stuffed into the description. Google expects a Business Profile for each location patients can visit. Practitioners can be attached to that location. Hours, phone, appointment URL, and services must match operations. Photos should look like the real clinic.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, How to create a Business Profile.

If two offices share a waiting room, that is still one location. If a provider only works Tuesdays in a second town, that town needs a staffed profile or it should not have one. Longer GBP guide: Google Business Profile for healthcare practices.

Citations that repeat the same NAP

Name, address (including suite), phone, hours, and core services should match across the website, GBP, and major directories. Tracking numbers that leak onto citations split the record. Payer directories and specialty associations matter as much as the general web. Use the directory profile audit for a short pass. Hygiene checklist: local SEO checklist for healthcare practices.

On-page for several providers

Each location needs unique copy: who that site takes, ages, payers, parking, and how to book. Do not clone one city page across five URLs. Schema should match visible text. Group pages can introduce the team. Location pages should still be able to rank and convert on their own. Definition: SEO for healthcare practices.

Reviews workflow the practice owns

Ask for reviews after a completed visit, on the correct location profile, without incentives that violate Google policies and without PHI in the public reply. The front desk owns the ask. Marketing can remind, template, and report. Fake stars are out. Workflow: review generation and reputation for healthcare practices.
