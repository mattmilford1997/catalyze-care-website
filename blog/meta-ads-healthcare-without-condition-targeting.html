Meta Ads for Healthcare: You Can't Target by Condition

By Andrew · Updated October 19, 2026·3 min read

Meta used to be the easy channel for healthcare: cheap reach, detailed targeting, fast creative testing. Then Meta removed detailed targeting options tied to health conditions, restricted what health advertisers can send through the pixel, and tightened review. It still works. It just works differently.

What you can't do anymore

  • Target by health condition or treatment. Interest categories like "anxiety," "ADHD," "autism awareness," and similar were removed for advertisers. You can't build an audience around a diagnosis.
  • Send health-related events through the pixel. Meta restricts data from websites it categorizes as health-related. Custom conversions tied to condition-specific pages may be blocked, and Meta may restrict your pixel entirely if it detects health data.
  • Use personalized health language in creative. "Struggling with depression?" gets rejected. "Do you have a child with a speech delay?" often does too.
  • Run addiction treatment ads without LegitScript certification. Same as Google.

What still works

Broad targeting plus creative that self-selects. Meta's algorithm is good at finding the right people if the creative is clear about who it's for. Target broadly by geography and age, and let a video or image that clearly describes "speech therapy for kids, covered by Medicaid, appointments this week" find the parents who need it. Creative does the targeting now.

Geography and life stage. Location, age, and parental status remain available. A pediatric therapy practice can target parents of young children in its service area without ever referencing a condition.

Lookalikes from first-party data. You can't build audiences from condition pages, but you can upload a hashed customer list (with appropriate HIPAA-compliant handling and no PHI beyond contact fields) and build lookalikes. This is often the best-performing audience.

Engagement audiences. People who engaged with your page, watched your videos, or interacted with your Instagram. Build these with content, then retarget.

Lead forms inside Meta. Instant forms keep the conversion on Meta's platform, which avoids pixel restrictions and often lowers cost per lead. Route submissions into a HIPAA-compliant CRM.

Creative that gets approved and converts

  • Describe the service and the practical benefits: availability, insurance accepted, telehealth or in-person, ages served.
  • Feature the clinician. Faces build trust and pass review.
  • Use plain language a parent or patient would use, without addressing them as though you know their condition.
  • Test short video (15 to 30 seconds) heavily. It outperforms static in most healthcare accounts we run.
  • Include a clear next step: book, call, or fill out the form.

Measurement without the pixel

If Meta restricts your pixel, you can still measure. Use instant forms for direct attribution. Use a dedicated landing page and phone number for Meta traffic. Use a server-side conversion setup that sends only non-health events (page views, form submits without content) through a HIPAA-conscious integration. Reconcile against your intake system monthly. Perfect attribution isn't available; directional attribution is, and it's enough to manage spend.

Where Meta fits in the mix

Google catches people searching for care right now. Meta reaches people who need care but aren't searching yet, and it reaches parents and caregivers who make decisions for someone else. For behavioral health, pediatric therapy, and services with awareness gaps, Meta often produces cheaper leads than Google at lower intent. Run it for volume and awareness; run Google for intent; run referrals for durability.
