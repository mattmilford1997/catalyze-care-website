Google Ads Account Structure for Healthcare Practices

By Andrew · Published September 11, 2026·10 min read

Google Ads account structure for a healthcare practice is a practice-owned account split into campaigns by intent and geography, with ad groups kept to one service theme so ads and landing pages match. Conversion events should measure booked inquiries the front desk can confirm—not diagnosis-level values in pixels or URLs. Six setup steps

  1. Put the account under the practice. Create or reclaim a Google Ads account the practice owns. The agency is a user, not the account holder. If they “bring their MCC and you never get admin,” you are renting. Contract questions: questions before signing a healthcare marketing contract.
  2. Split campaigns by intent and geography. Keep brand separate from specialty-plus-location search. Give any category that needs its own certification or policy review its own campaign so a disapproval does not pause the whole account. Multi-location practices should not run one catch-all campaign for every office.
  3. Keep ad groups to one service theme. One theme per ad group so headlines and the landing page describe the same service. Do not mix unrelated specialties in one ad group. Tight themes also make negative keywords easier to maintain.
  4. Match the landing page to the ad. Describe the service, avoid personalized health language (“your anxiety”), and carry a privacy policy. Destination mismatches are a common disapproval. Policy and certification: Google Ads, LegitScript, and healthcare disapprovals.
  5. Set conversion events without PHI. Measure booked inquiries the front desk can confirm. No diagnosis-level values in pixels or URL parameters. HHS OCR has published tracking-technology guidance for covered entities. This page is not legal advice. How-to: HIPAA-aware Google Ads conversion tracking.
  6. Launch small and watch the policy tab. Finish advertiser verification—and any LegitScript or category certification—before you scale. Watch the policy tab daily in the first two weeks. Catalyze Care’s published management fee is $2,500/month; media is billed separately.

HIPAA-aware notes that change structure

  • Do not build remarketing lists from clinical page visits. Condition-based pools are restricted. Prefer first-party booked-inquiry events on public, generic thank-you pages.
  • Inventory every tag (Ads, Analytics, call tracking, chat) before assuming “the agency handled HIPAA.”
  • If you run a health app outside classic covered-entity rules, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule may still apply. Do not assume “we’re not a covered entity” means tracking is unconstrained.

Source: HHS, HIPAA and marketing. Source: FTC, Complying with the FTC’s Health Breach Notification Rule.
