The First 90 Days of a Referral Campaign

By Andrew Brazis · Updated October 2, 2026·6 min read

The first 90 days of a healthcare referral campaign are list, first wave, second wave, and nurture. Days 1 to 14 build the list and tracking. Days 15 to 30 send the first touches. Days 31 to 60 follow up with offices that responded and offices that did not. Days 61 to 90 close the loop and cut segments that are not responding. In one sentenceJudge month one on delivery and office response; judge the quarter on distinct referring offices and second referrals, not on a promised return.

Days 1 to 14: list and launch

The list is built, scored, and scrubbed. Creative is finalized: fax-back form, voicemail script, LinkedIn message, email. Tracking is set up: a dedicated phone number and fax line so you can attribute inbound activity to the campaign.

What to count: list size by segment, and which numbers failed to deliver. Clean undeliverable fax numbers and phone numbers as they fail.

Days 15 to 30: first wave

Every target office gets the first touches: fax, then voicemail three to five days later, then LinkedIn or email where contacts exist.

What to count:

  • Any response (callback, fax-back, reply, or form). Write down which specialties and places responded.
  • Offices that sent a patient. A few may. Most will not have responded yet.

What good looks like: a handful of offices have called to ask how to refer. You can see which specialties are responding. Most offices have not responded yet.

Days 31 to 60: second wave and conversion

Offices that have not responded get a second fax with a different angle (a specific service or a capacity update) and a second voicemail. Offices that responded get a personal follow-up: a call or visit, referral materials, and a direct contact.

What to count:

  • Referring offices. Count offices that have sent at least one patient. Count offices, not only patients.
  • Time to first visit for referred patients. If the wait is long, referring offices notice and stop.

What good looks like: you can name the offices that sent a patient, and you can see which segments to keep.

Days 61 to 90: nurture and scale

Referring offices get a thank-you, a note that the consult went back, and a clear way to send the next patient. High-fit offices that still have not responded get another touch. Segments that are not responding come off the list. Segments that are responding get a longer list.

What to count:

  • Second referrals. Which offices that sent one patient sent another?
  • Booked appointments from intake. Match them to the referring office, not to a platform click.

What good looks like: you can name the offices to keep, and you know which segments to expand next quarter.

Specialty, payer mix, and how fast your intake team answers will move these fields. Read them against your own baseline. Closed-loop coordination is why referring offices keep sending: CMS, Care Coordination and AHRQ, Care Coordination.

The three things that break campaigns

  1. Slow intake. The referring office calls, nobody answers, or the patient waits three weeks. The office stops referring.
  2. No closed loop. The referring provider never hears what happened. They forget you exist.
  3. One wave and done. Offices often need more than one touch before they respond. A single fax is a test, not a campaign.
