The First 90 Days of a Referral Campaign

By Andrew · Updated September 9, 2026·4 min read

The first 90 days of a healthcare referral campaign are list, first wave, second wave, and nurture—not a week-two patient promise. Days 1–14 build and scrub the list and tracking. Days 15–30 send the first multi-channel touches. Days 31–60 convert responders and re-touch non-responders. Days 61–90 close the loop and cut weak segments. In one sentenceJudge month one on delivery and office response; judge the quarter on distinct referring offices and second referrals—not on invented ROI.

Days 1 to 14: list and launch

The list is built, scored, and scrubbed. Creative is finalized: fax-back form, voicemail script, LinkedIn message, email. Tracking is set up: a dedicated phone number and fax line so you can attribute inbound activity to the campaign.

Metric: list size by segment, and delivery rates. Faxes should deliver at 90 percent or better; undeliverable numbers are cleaned immediately.

Days 15 to 30: first wave

Every target office gets the first touches: fax, then voicemail three to five days later, then LinkedIn or email where contacts exist.

Metrics to watch:

  • Response rate (any inbound: callback, fax-back, reply, form submission). Well-targeted campaigns typically see 5 to 12 percent of offices respond to the first wave. Below 3 percent usually means a list or messaging problem.
  • Response by segment. By day 30, you can usually see which specialties and geographies are responding.

What good looks like: a handful of offices have called to ask how to refer. One or two have sent a patient. Most haven't responded yet, which is normal.

Days 31 to 60: second wave and conversion

Non-responders get a second fax with a different angle (a specific service, a capacity update, an outcome statistic) and a second voicemail. Responders get a personal follow-up: a call or visit to the office, referral materials, and a direct contact.

Metrics to watch:

  • Cumulative response rate. Second waves typically add 3 to 6 points, bringing well-run campaigns to 8 to 15 percent by day 60.
  • Referring offices. Count offices that have sent at least one patient. Not patients, offices. The number of distinct referring offices is the leading indicator of a durable channel.
  • Time to first visit for referred patients. If it's more than a week, referring offices notice and stop.

What good looks like: 5 to 15 referring offices per market, a steady trickle of referred patients, and a clear picture of which segments to double down on.

Days 61 to 90: nurture and scale

Referring offices get the first nurture touch: a thank-you, a consult note turnaround confirmation, and an ask for more. High-fit non-responders get a third touch. Underperforming segments are cut; overperforming ones get expanded lists.

Metrics to watch:

  • Patients per referring office per month. The top offices should be sending two or more.
  • Cost per referred patient. Total campaign cost divided by patients from referred offices. By day 90 this is usually already competitive with paid CAC and falling.
  • Second-referral rate. What share of offices that sent one patient sent a second? This is the metric that predicts the channel's long-term value.

What good looks like: referred patient volume is growing month over month, the top ten offices are identifiable, and you know exactly which segments to target in the next market.

The three things that break campaigns

  1. Slow intake. The referring office calls, nobody answers, or the patient waits three weeks. The office stops referring.
  2. No closed loop. The referring provider never hears what happened. They forget you exist.
  3. One wave and done. Most responses come on touches two through four. A single fax is a test, not a campaign.

FAQ

When should a practice expect referred patients?

Not in week one. The first two weeks are list, creative, and tracking. First-wave responses show up in days 15–30. Durable referring offices are a quarter-scale metric. Ranges on this page are planning context, not a guarantee.

What metrics matter in the first 90 days?

Delivery rate, office response rate, distinct referring offices, time to first visit, and second-referral rate. Count offices that sent a patient, not just leads.
