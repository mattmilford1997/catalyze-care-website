SEO Audit Checklist for Healthcare Practices

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 27, 2026·9 min read

An SEO audit for a healthcare practice checks the four things that decide whether patients find you in Google: your Google Business Profile, whether Google can crawl and index your site, the service and location pages patients land on, and the reviews and directory listings that confirm you are real. A single-location practice can run the first pass in an afternoon with Google Search Console, the Business Profile dashboard, and a phone. Work through the list in order, because fixing a wrong phone number on your listing matters more than speeding up a blog image.

In one sentenceAudit the listing first, then indexing, then the pages patients land on, then reviews and citations, and turn what you find into a 90-day fix list with one owner per item.

1. Start with your Google Business Profile

For searches like a specialty plus a city, the map results often show before any website. Open your Business Profile and confirm each item below.

  • Ownership. The profile sits in a Google account the practice controls, with the agency or staff added as managers.
  • Primary category. It names your main service in plain terms, such as pediatric dentist or physical therapy clinic.
  • Phone and hours. The number rings at scheduling, and holiday hours are set before the holiday.
  • Website link. For groups with several offices, each profile links to its own location page on your site.
  • One profile per staffed location. Duplicates split reviews and confuse the map. Our guide to local SEO for multi-provider groups covers cleanup.

Google publishes the rules in its guidelines for representing your business. Read them once before you change a name or category.

2. Check what Google can crawl and index

Search Console shows which of your pages Google has indexed and why others were left out. Check these in the Pages and Sitemaps reports.

  • A sitemap is submitted and Search Console reads it without errors.
  • Every core service and location page shows as indexed.
  • One version of the site loads: https, with or without www, and the others redirect to it.
  • Pages read cleanly on a phone with no sideways scrolling.
  • The Core Web Vitals report has no pages stuck in the poor group.

Google's SEO starter guide explains each report in plain language.

3. Read your service and location pages like a patient

Open each core page on your phone and ask whether a parent or patient could book from it in under a minute.

  • Each main service has its own page, with a title that names the service and the city.
  • The first paragraph says who you see, where, and how to book.
  • Phone and booking buttons are visible without scrolling.
  • Provider names, credentials, and locations are on the page.
  • A clinician has reviewed any clinical statement.

Provider and location pages carry a lot of weight here. Our walkthrough on provider and location pages that rank lists what each one needs.

4. Confirm reviews and listings tell the same story

Patients and search engines cross-check your name, address, phone, and hours across Google, Healthgrades, Zocdoc, WebMD, and insurer directories. Mismatches send patients to old addresses. Run the 30-minute directory profile audit, then set up an honest review request after completed visits.

5. Look at the searches you already appear for

In the Search Console Performance report, sort queries by impressions and look at average position. Searches where you already sit near the bottom of page one or the top of page two are often the quickest to improve, usually by tightening the page title, the first paragraph, and the internal links that point to that page.

6. Turn the audit into a 90-day fix list

  1. Fix blockers first. Unverified listings, wrong phone numbers, pages left out of the index.
  2. Improve pages that almost rank. Tighten titles, first paragraphs, and internal links.
  3. Clean up listings and reviews. Match details everywhere and start a steady review ask.
  4. Add missing pages last. New services, providers, or locations that have no page yet.

FAQ

How often should a healthcare practice run an SEO audit?

Run a full audit once or twice a year and a quick monthly check of your Google Business Profile details and Search Console coverage. Run it again after a move, a new location, a new provider, or a website rebuild.

What tools do I need for a practice SEO audit?

Google Search Console, your Google Business Profile dashboard, and a smartphone. Paid crawlers help on large sites, but a single-location practice can find most issues with the free tools.

Can an SEO audit tell me when I will rank?

An audit shows what is blocking visibility and what to fix first. Timing depends on competition in your specialty and city, so treat any fixed ranking date as a warning sign.
