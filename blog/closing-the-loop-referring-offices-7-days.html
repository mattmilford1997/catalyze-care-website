Closing the Loop With Referring Offices in 7 Days

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 26, 2026·9 min read

Closing the loop with referring offices in 7 days means a named owner tells the sending office what happened after each referral (received, scheduled, seen, or needs more info) inside one week using fax, email, or portal. Why seven days

Referring offices stop sending when they do not hear back. Seven days is a practice SLA you can staff, not a platform promise. Shorter is better when the case is urgent. Longer needs an explicit reason in the note. Analytics view: referral analytics and closing the loop.

Who owns the SLA

  • Named seat. Liaison, referral coordinator, or front-desk lead. One name on the monthly report.
  • Backup. Who covers vacation so the seventh day still fires.
  • Intake link. Scheduling must confirm the appointment before you tell the sender "scheduled."

Staff capacity before scale: staff intake before scaling referral or paid.

Channel: fax, email, or portal

Use the channel the referring office already monitors. Many still prefer fax for clinical packets. Email works when the office gave you a monitored inbox. Portals help when both sides already live there. Do not add a new tool mid-quarter unless both offices agree. Fax outreach context: fax marketing for physician offices. Nurture cadence: referral nurture cadence for referring offices.

What the 7-day note should say

  1. Patient initials or office reference only (follow your privacy rules).
  2. Status: received, needs info, scheduled (date), seen, or declined with reason category.
  3. Who to call with questions (direct line).
  4. Thanks for the send, without marketing fluff.

Fields on the monthly packet

Outreach volume, meetings, referrals received, booked appointments, and close-the-loop latency in days. Those are fields to ask for, not a conversion rate promise. First ninety days: referral campaign first 90 days benchmarks. Firm overview: healthcare marketing agency.

How to install the 7-day SLA

  1. Name the owner and backup. Write both on the referral report header.
  2. Pick the default channel per office. Fax, email, or portal. Record it on the target list.
  3. Template the status note. Four lines max. Counsel review if clinical detail is included.
  4. Measure latency. Days from referral received to status sent. Review weekly for the first month.
