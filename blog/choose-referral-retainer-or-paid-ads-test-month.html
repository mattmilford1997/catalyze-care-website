How to Choose Between a Referral Retainer and a Paid Ads Test Month

By Andrew Brazis · Updated September 20, 2026·9 min read

Choose a referral retainer when other offices already send this specialty and you can keep the slot you promise. Choose a paid ads test month when openings need nearer-term demand and intake can book the next new patient. Catalyze Care publishes referral marketing at $5,000/month (3-month minimum) and paid ads management at $2,500/month plus media. SEO at $799/month is compounding work, not the first-month test. Service pages: referral and paid ads. Hire timing: when not to hire a healthcare marketing agency.

In one sentenceReferral at $5,000 when offices already send the specialty. Paid at $2,500 plus media when openings need nearer-term demand. Hire neither if intake is full.

Decision tree

  1. Check intake first. If the front desk cannot book the next new patient in a time you will honor, hire neither channel yet. Capacity: staff intake before scaling referral or paid.
  2. Ask who already sends this specialty. If other offices already send patients, start the $5,000 referral retainer. A single month is not a test. The minimum is three months. Service: referral. Lander: healthcare referral marketing.
  3. Ask whether openings need nearer-term demand. If patients search and you have openings now, start a $2,500 paid ads management month plus media. Service: paid ads. Lander: paid ads for healthcare practices.
  4. Write pause and cancel terms before you sign. Ask in writing what happens if intake fills or a platform policy blocks the campaign. Pause tests: when to pause paid ads vs SEO vs referral. Process: how Catalyze Care works.

FAQ

When should a practice start a referral retainer?

Start referral at $5,000/month with a 3-month minimum when other offices already send this specialty and intake can keep the slot you promise a referring office. A single month is not a test.

When should a practice start a paid ads test month?

Start paid ads at $2,500/month management plus media when openings need nearer-term demand and intake can book the next new patient. Pause if policy or license facts break.

When should a practice hire neither?

Do not hire when intake cannot take the next patient, when the practice will not name who it takes, or when the only ask is a guaranteed ranking or multiple. Honesty: when not to hire a healthcare marketing agency.
