Close-the-Loop Referral Reporting for Practices

By Andrew · Published September 11, 2026·8 min read

Close-the-loop referral reporting for a healthcare practice is a monthly packet the administrator can check against intake: who referred each new patient, which offices were contacted, which offices are new or fading, and whether the sending office received a consult note after the first visit. It is not a hospital leakage dashboard and it does not invent a leakage percentage. Catalyze Care referral marketing is published at $5,000/month with a 3-month minimum. Why “close the loop” is a report, not a slogan

Referring offices keep sending when they hear what happened. CMS and AHRQ treat care coordination as a clinical expectation, not a marketing flourish. A monthly report that only lists faxes sent is activity. A monthly report that also shows whether a first-visit note went back to the sending office is close-the-loop reporting.

Six monthly measures (no invented leakage %)

  1. Require a referring-provider field. Intake captures a named office or “self-referred,” not a free-text “internet” blob. If the field is optional, the rest of the report is guesswork.
  2. Count outreach the practice can verify. Offices contacted, channel (fax, email, phone, LinkedIn, mail), and responses. Delivery problems belong on the report.
  3. List new and fading referrers. First-time senders this period, and offices that sent before and did not send this period. Those are names from your EHR, not a market-share invention.
  4. Match booked inquiries to intake. Referred patients who scheduled, using the practice’s own calendar. If the number cannot be checked, it does not belong on the slide. Process: how Catalyze Care works.
  5. Log the consult-note loop. Whether a summary went back to the sending office after the first visit. Count completions from your workflow. Do not invent a market leakage rate to stand in for that count.
  6. Tag the campaign wave. When an office refers, you know which outreach wave they sat on. Attribution is a tag, not an ROI multiple.

Leakage is a word, not a Catalyze metric

Leakage is a useful word for “patients who could have come here and went elsewhere.” Claims-backed referral intelligence can show which offices in a market send this specialty. Compare that list to offices that have sent you patients. The gap is an outreach list. It is not a published leakage percentage on this site.
