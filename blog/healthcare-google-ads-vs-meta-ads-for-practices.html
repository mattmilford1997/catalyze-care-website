Google Ads vs Meta Ads for a healthcare practice

By Andrew Brazis · Updated October 9, 2026·8 min read

Google Ads catch people who are already searching for your specialty and location. Meta Ads reach people who are not searching yet, with creative that names the service and how to book, without targeting a diagnosis. Catalyze Care manages either channel, or both, for $2,500 a month, and media spend is billed separately. See how we run both on paid ads for healthcare practices.

In one sentenceUse Google Ads when people are searching for care, and Meta Ads when you need awareness, with creative and tracking that stay inside healthcare ad rules.

When Google Ads fit

Search ads fit when patients type the care and the place: a therapist in a city, a psychiatry practice that takes a payer, a specialty with open slots this month. The click arrives with a question. The landing page should answer that question: who you see, the insurance you take, and a phone number or form that intake can handle.

Google also reviews healthcare ads under its healthcare and medicines policy, and some categories need extra certification before ads run. Its personalized advertising rules list health as a restricted category, so remarketing and customer lists cannot be built around a health condition. Build the account around services and locations you actually offer, and send each ad to a page that matches the search. Call tracking should count conversations your front desk can confirm, not every hang-up.

When Meta Ads fit

Meta fits when the people you want are not typing a query. Parents planning a school transition, adults who know they want therapy and have not searched yet, or a community that should know a new office opened. The ad has to earn the click with the creative, because there is no search term to match.

Meta removed detailed targeting options tied to health causes in 2022, and its advertising standards do not allow ads that assert or imply a person's physical or mental health. Use geography, age where the platform allows it, and creative that lets the right person self-select. Do not build an audience from a diagnosis, a medication, or a patient list. Meta Ads for healthcare without condition targeting covers the practical limits.

Creative that stays HIPAA-aware

Write the ad and the landing page so a stranger could read both without learning a patient's condition. Name the service line, ages, insurance, location, and the next step. Leave diagnosis, symptoms that identify a person, and member IDs off the ad, the URL, and the thank-you page.

Pixels belong on public pages and a generic thank-you URL. Patient portals and diagnosis-specific success pages stay untagged. The same rules apply if you remarket: public pages only, and exclude people who already booked without passing health information to the platform. Do Google Ads and Meta Ads share one landing page?
