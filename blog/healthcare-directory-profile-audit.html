Your Directory Profiles Are Losing You Patients: A 30-Minute Audit

By Andrew · Updated December 14, 2026·3 min read

Before a patient calls you, they've probably seen you on Google, on an insurance directory, and on at least one specialty site. Before a referring office sends a patient, someone there has checked that you take the patient's plan. If any of those sources is wrong, you lose the patient and never know it. Here's a fast audit.

1 Minutes 0 to 5: Google Business Profile

Search your practice name and look at the panel.

  • Is the name exactly your legal or DBA name, with no keywords stuffed in? (Keyword-stuffed names get suspended.)
  • Are hours correct, including holiday hours?
  • Is the phone number the one your intake team actually answers?
  • Is the category the most specific one available (not "medical clinic" if "speech pathologist" exists)?
  • Are there recent reviews, and have you responded to them?
  • Are services listed, with descriptions?
  • Is there a booking link?

Fix anything wrong today. This profile drives more calls than your website for most local searches.

2 Minutes 5 to 12: insurance directories

Pull up your listing in each payer's provider directory for your top five plans, including each Medicaid managed care plan in your states.

  • Are you listed at all?
  • Is the specialty correct?
  • Is the location or telehealth status correct?
  • Is "accepting new patients" set to yes?
  • Are all of your clinicians listed, or just the group?

Payer directories are notoriously wrong, and the fix requires contacting the payer's provider relations. Start the tickets now; they take weeks. This is where referral coordinators verify you, so errors here cost referrals directly.

3 Minutes 12 to 20: specialty directories

For behavioral health: Psychology Today, and any others that produce patients in your market. For medical: Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Vitals. For pediatric therapy: the specialty-specific directories parents use and the state early-intervention or Medicaid provider lists.

For each clinician profile on the ones that matter:

  • Does the headline say who you help and how, in plain language, in the first line?
  • Is insurance accurate, especially Medicaid plans?
  • Is availability current? (Profiles marked "not accepting" or with stale availability get skipped.)
  • Is there a photo that looks like a person you'd want to talk to?
  • Does the profile link to the right booking path?

Psychology Today profiles in particular reward specific, warm, first-person writing over clinical boilerplate. Rewriting a weak profile often doubles inquiries from that source.

4 Minutes 20 to 25: consistency check

Compare name, address, phone, and website across Google, your site, and the top three directories. Any mismatch (old suite number, old phone, "Inc." on one and not another) weakens local rankings and confuses patients. Pick one canonical version and propagate it.

5 Minutes 25 to 30: the referral-office view

Now do what a pediatric office manager does. Google "[your specialty] [your city] Medicaid." Do you appear? Click through. Can she tell in 10 seconds whether you take the plan and how to refer? If not, that's the fix that matters most.

What to do quarterly

Re-run the audit. Directories drift: a clinician leaves, a plan changes, a payer re-loads its directory from bad data. Practices that check quarterly catch it. Practices that don't find out when a referral coordinator says "we thought you weren't taking Medicaid anymore."
